    On a solar farm

    Rows of trackers shade each other, so they deliberately point away from the sun

    Everything above is one tracker standing alone. A solar farm packs trackers in rows, and when the sun is low each row's shadow lands on the next. Farms handle this with backtracking: the rows turn back toward flat just enough that no shadow reaches the neighbor, giving up some direct sunlight to stay out of the shade.7 Move the time to early morning and switch between the two strategies.

    How it works

    The same physics as the full model, small enough for a browser

    Every hour of the year, the page finds the sun with the NOAA solar equations,35 then splits the light on each panel into the direct beam, sky light using the Perez model,10 and light reflected off the ground. It removes what the glass reflects,11,12 works out how hot the cells get with Sandia's model,13 and converts to DC and AC power with the PVWatts equations and 14.08% system losses.16 The panels and inverter use real specifications from the CEC database.17

    The one simplification: the full model uses a detailed single diode model for the panel and Sandia's inverter model,15 while this page uses the lighter PVWatts equations for both. Between hours, the weather is interpolated from the hourly averages.
